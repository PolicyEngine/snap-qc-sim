# Methodology review: "Administrative quality control data as an oracle" (paper/index.qmd)

## Recommendation: Major revisions

The core machinery is unusually well built for a policy paper — I verified the closed-form moment and survival calculations, the nested cross-fitting is genuinely leakage-free, and the artifact discipline (FACTS.md, generated FINDINGS.md, staged deterministic reruns) is exemplary. But the manuscript contains two direction-of-effect misstatements that contradict its own committed artifacts, and the deployed simulator's observed mode — the source of the headline tier-flip and dollar numbers — is built on the exact error definition the paper's own audit banned. All are fixable; none can ship as-is.

---

## Assessment by area (the brief's seven questions)

**1. Model stack.** The hurdle structure, OOF isotonic calibration, quantile GBMs with per-case rearrangement, and OOF Duan smear are sound and correctly implemented (`analysis/hurdle_deviation_model.py`, `analysis/distributional_deviation_model.py`, `analysis/predictive_process.py`). I checked the math in `predictive_process.py` line by line: the piecewise log-linear segment integral, the survival inversion with point masses at flat segments, the Pareto-in-dollars tail moment E[e^E] = 1/(1−σ), and the truncated-tail partial expectation are all correct, with closed-form unit tests (`tests/test_predictive_process.py`). The finite-mean condition (σ < 1) is enforced in both `_fit_tail` and `expected_error_dollars` and used correctly. The tail *estimation*, however, has an internal mismatch (Major 6). Fitting the exponential scale on top-decile OOF residuals is defensible in spirit (OOF avoids optimistically small boosted-model residuals — same logic as the OOF smear, which is arguably better than Duan's original in-sample version), but the construction quietly assumes a location-shift error model that the non-parallel quantile fits contradict.

**2. Temporal protocol.** Genuinely out-of-sample in the estimation sense. I traced the frozen path: model fit on {2017–19, 2022} only, FY2023 predictions from that model produce the factors, EB-shrunk, frozen, applied to FY2024 (`hurdle_deviation_model.py:1002–1012`). Calibrators are fit on training OOF scores only; FY2024 outcomes never touch any fit. Two residual concerns: `year` is a numeric feature that saturates at the last training year (HistGB bins put 2024 in the ≤2023 bin), so the model structurally cannot express drift — consistent with the +0.05 logit under-prediction on FY2024 stage 1; and FY2024 was the evaluation year throughout the audit-and-correct development loop, so it is temporally out-of-sample but not a lockbox (Major 7).

**3. Validation suite.** Weighted marginal PIT coverage at nine levels is a valid necessary check but weak: it tests average calibration only, not conditional calibration, and says nothing about cross-case dependence, which is what the rate-SD outputs actually need. The flagged 3.5/3.3pp misses matter for the simulator mainly through the SD, and the paper misdescribes their direction (Major 1). The model-vs-bootstrap mean comparison is fair (both recentered identically; means are comparable); the SD columns are not comparable as constructed (Minor 12).

**4. Level anchoring.** Anchoring the model mode at the official rate is defensible for the mean — it is standard benchmarking to a known total, is disclosed in the tool (`app/public/app.js:306–307, 375–384`, shift = official − model baseline mean), and the paper says so. The artifacts it introduces are not stated: (a) it corrects location only — NY's model SD is 0.616pp vs 0.946pp bootstrap, so model-mode tier probabilities for underpredicted states inherit a spread ~35% too tight; (b) the additive shift can breach support (negative rates possible for low-rate states; no clipping in `snap_qc_sim/simulate.py` or `app.js`); (c) it silently absorbs two distinct gaps — model-vs-sample and sample-vs-official (the FNS regression adjustment), which for NY is enormous (file-derived 7.84% vs official 14.09%, per `analysis/hurdle_results.json` state tables).

**5. SMD treatment.** The design discipline is genuinely good — post-treatment conditioning named, both denominators reported, unweighted event counts shown, calendar-aligned windows, the dissolved Kentucky estimate disclosed. But the manuscript's cross-sectional sentence reverses its own artifact (Major 2).

**6. Survey weighting.** Consistent and careful throughout: HWGT in training losses, metrics, isotonic fits, coverage, permutation importance; Kish effective n in shrinkage variances; delta-method ratio variances. Best-in-class for this literature. Small exceptions in Minors 10 and 14.

**7. Bootstrap vs the QC design.** This is the weakest methodological link given that the headline claim is precisely about sampling noise (Major 4).

## Major issues

**1. The coverage misses are misdescribed — direction reversed, and the one-sided pattern is unreported.** (§sec-model, "Validation"; artifact: `analysis/distributional_results.json`, `analysis/FINDINGS.md` coverage table.) The paper says the 75th and 90th percentiles "over-cover by 3.5 and 3.3 points — the model slightly overstates mid-upper magnitudes." The artifact shows weighted coverage 71.53% and 86.75% against nominal 75% and 90%: gaps of −3.47 and −3.25pp. That is *under*-coverage; the predicted quantiles are too low; the model *understates* mid-upper magnitudes (consistent with predicted $183 vs observed $189, and with the NY level gap). FACTS.md E3 records the negative signs correctly; the manuscript flips both words. Worse, all nine gaps are negative (−0.30 to −3.47) — a one-sided systematic miscalibration, not noise around nominal. "Within 3 points at seven of nine levels" obscures this. Report the signed gaps, show a PIT histogram, and connect the one-sided understatement to its consequence: under-drawn magnitudes compress the simulated rate distribution, which is the quantity the tool sells.

**2. The SMD cross-sectional contrast is reversed against the committed artifact.** (§sec-model, "Policy contrasts"; artifact: `analysis/model_results.json` `medical_cross_section`, `analysis/train_error_model.py:427–437`.) The paper: "States that operate a standard medical deduction show slightly lower medical-error event rates in cross-section (2.60% versus 2.97%...)." In the code, `med_doc_required = above_$35_floor & elderly_disabled & ~SMD-state`; the JSON assigns 2.60% to the `med_doc_required=1` cell — claimants in states *without* an SMD — and 2.97% to the `=0` cell (SMD-state claimants pooled with below-floor claimants everywhere). Two errors: the direction is reversed (the artifact runs *against* the burden-reduction hypothesis in cross-section), and neither cell is "states that operate an SMD" — the 0-cell is a mixture. FACTS E7 states it correctly; the manuscript does not. This is exactly the class of error the paper's G-section prohibitions exist to catch.

**3. The deployed observed-mode simulator — the source of headline results F1–F4 — is built on the error definition the paper's own audit banned, on the wrong case universe.** (§sec-simulate; `snap_qc_sim/data.py:84–101` `load_cases`; `scripts_build_data.py`; `app/public/data.json`.) The app data gate is `STATUS ∈ {2,3} AND |RAWBEN − FSBEN| > $56` (and FSBEN nonmissing), with no `CASE == 1` filter — the per-state n's in the shipped `data.json` sum to **44,891**, the full file, not the 44,800 official universe. The paper's correction round (footnote [^audit]; FACTS B3, G) established that FSBEN-based deviation "conflates legitimate adjustments such as proration with error" (|RAWBEN−FSBEN| = AMTERR for only 83.65% of weighted cases), and the analysis pipeline plus `tests/test_analysis_pipeline.py::test_official_label_uses_status_and_amterr_not_benefit_differences` enshrine the corrected label — yet the tier-flip probabilities (ND 49%, etc.), the CO $156M/$33M figures, the audit-volume dollar effects, and the $606M/$1.31B accounting bounds all flow from the FSBEN-gated loader (confirmed: `app.js` all-states table and `paper/snapshot/labs/mc_results_by_state.json` both derive from this data layer). Official-rate recentering absorbs level error, but the error-case *set*, per-case error dollars, lever-category element sums, and bootstrap SDs all inherit the wrong gate; true errors whose FSBEN difference is ≤ $56 drop out entirely, and cases with AMTERR ≤ $56 can enter. Rebuild `data.json` on the corrected gate (`AMTERR > 56`) and `CASE == 1`, regenerate F1–F4, or quantify the discrepancy and disclose it. As it stands the paper's two halves use different error definitions.

**4. The observed-resample mode does not emulate the estimator whose noise it prices, and is never checked against a design-based SE.** (§sec-simulate.) Three gaps, in order of importance: (a) The official rate is regression-adjusted using the federal rereview subsample. Centering at the official rate while resampling only state-found error dollars treats the adjustment as a known constant with zero variance and zero covariance with the sample. For NY the file-derived error-dollar rate is 7.84% against an official 14.09% — the bootstrap resamples a numerator roughly half the size the official estimator implies, which plausibly understates the SD for high-adjustment states roughly in proportion; and the rereview-estimation variance component is ignored for every state. (b) The iid case bootstrap ignores the 7 CFR 275.11 stratified monthly systematic design; proportionate month stratification generally *reduces* design variance relative to the iid approximation, biasing the SD the other way. (c) No comparison to any design-based variance (FNS's regression-estimator methodology has one; historical liability determinations used formal state-rate tolerances). The paper's only stated assumption — "the sample representing the state's case mix" — materially understates what is being assumed. The coin-flip conclusion is robust for CO/KS (0.02–0.03pp from a boundary — any nonzero SD gives ~50%), but the specific 47–49% figures for ND/WA/NV, and all audit-volume dollar magnitudes, are SD-sensitive. A hostile referee will demand at minimum a bracketing analysis (with/without a rereview-variance term; a month-stratified bootstrap) and a reconciliation against FNS's own variance machinery.

**5. Model-mode draws assume cross-case independence that the paper's own decomposition contradicts, and anchoring corrects the mean but not the spread.** (§sec-model, §sec-simulate.) Layer 1 documents systemic causes — computer programming, mass-change processing — that by construction hit many cases in the same state-period simultaneously. Independent per-case draws given features therefore understate the variance (and right tail) of the measured rate: exactly the output the model mode exists to produce. Combined with the one-sided magnitude under-coverage (Major 1) and the NY SD gap (0.616 vs 0.946pp), the model mode's tier probabilities are compression-biased for high-error states even after anchoring. State this limitation where the anchoring choice is stated, and consider a state-period random effect as the obvious next component.

**6. The tail fit is internally inconsistent with the quantile model and carries no diagnostics.** (`analysis/distributional_deviation_model.py:_fit_tail`.) The scale is the weighted mean excess of OOF *median-model* residuals beyond the residual q90, attached beyond each case's conditional q99. This (i) equates exceedances over two different thresholds, valid only if the excess distribution is memoryless (the thing being assumed); (ii) presumes a homoskedastic location-shift model — parallel conditional quantiles — while the nine separately fit quantile curves are allowed to be non-parallel, so the global scale under-tails high-dispersion cases and over-tails tight ones; (iii) is reported with no threshold-stability check (scale at q85/q90/q95 cutoffs), no mean-excess or QQ plot, and no SE. Additionally, σ = 0.467 sits just under the 0.5 boundary below which the dollar tail has finite *variance*; the paper cites only the finite-mean condition (correctly used), but the reported SDs and 4,000-draw Monte Carlo stability depend on the variance condition, which deserves explicit mention and a sensitivity band. Finally, draws are unbounded above — nothing caps |D| at any physically possible deviation (e.g., relative to the maximum allotment), so rare draws can exceed any realizable case error.

**7. FY2024 is a development-reused test year, and the paper should say so.** The footnoted audit narrative makes clear that targets, universes, features, and the calibration protocol were all revised while FY2024 metrics were observable. Temporal out-of-sample is not the same as untouched; the reported FY2024 AUCs/MAEs carry some selection optimism of unknowable size. One sentence of acknowledgment, plus a commitment to a frozen-pipeline FY2025 confirmation, resolves this.

## Minor issues

1. **Mismatched model pairs in the calibration sentence** (§sec-model): the unfactored 1.83/1.45pp MAEs are the *primary* model (trained through 2023); the factored 0.885/0.785pp are the *frozen* model (through 2022). The matched frozen unfactored baselines are 1.810/1.647pp (`FINDINGS.md`). Quote the matched pair.
2. Only the factored cross-state correlation (0.906) is reported; unfactored is 0.507 equal / 0.458 issuance-weighted. Most cross-state alignment comes from the frozen factors, as the paper's own caveat implies — report both.
3. **Unit mixing in the decomposition synthesis** (§sec-decompose): "roughly 3–10% of error dollars by layers 1–2, and bounded above by 13.1% in the replay" — 13.1% is 37/283 unweighted filtered *cases*; layers 1–2 are weighted *dollar* shares. Say "of filtered cases" explicitly.
4. **F1 traceability**: quoted CO 47% and NV 47% match neither committed snapshot (`mc_results_by_state.json`: CO 48.5%, NV 45.9%; `tier_noise_monte_carlo.json`: CO 48.0%, NV 45.8%). MC-seed/rounding drift between text and artifact violates the FACTS discipline the paper advertises.
5. §sec-qc states errors are recorded only "when they differ by more than a tolerance threshold," but the file contains STATUS 2/3 findings below $56 (e.g., an AMTERR=$10 case in `paper/snapshot/labs/phase_a_classification.json`), and the 305-case/$112.6M decomposition universe appears to include them. State whether the tolerance was applied to the decomposition numerator, since the 8.88% is compared to the official 9.97%.
6. AUC reporting mixes raw (stages 1–2: 0.836, 0.723) and calibrated (sign: 0.700) values; standardize.
7. Deployment calibrators are fit on OOF fold-model scores but applied to the full-refit model's scores — standard, but the score-distribution shift merits one sentence. (The nested outer/inner design used for reported training metrics is genuinely leakage-free — and tested, which is rare.)
8. EB shrinkage prior mean is fixed at 1 despite a documented net level under-prediction; a precision-weighted grand-mean prior is more standard, and factor uncertainty is not propagated into FY2024.
9. Additive anchoring can produce negative rate draws for low-rate states; clip at zero or use a location-scale/multiplicative correction.
10. `load_cases` maps missing HWGT to 0.0; zero-weight rows occupy bootstrap slots (with the 91 non-universe rows), mildly distorting effective resample size.
11. Per-case monotonization is rearrangement — cite Chernozhukov, Fernández-Val and Galichon (2010); Koenker–Bassett covers the loss only.
12. The model-vs-bootstrap table compares fixed-roster process variance (model rows) against composition-only variance (bootstrap rows); means are comparable, SDs are not, and the deployed app's model mode includes both components. Label the SD columns; the paper wisely quotes only means, but FINDINGS invites the SD comparison.
13. The sign model (AUC 0.700) contributes nothing to any reported output (rates use |D|); justify its presence (over/under decomposition later) or move it to the roadmap.
14. "Three and a half times chance" (47.8% vs 13.4%): fine, but the 5%-budget precision has no SE, and QC-sampled cases are not the operational targeting population — one hedging clause.
15. `simulate()` at hypothetical n + extra assumes new reviews share the realized disposition/nonresponse profile; worth stating next to the case-mix assumption.

## Strengths

1. **The oracle framing with a zero-tolerance bar and an honest scope caveat** (§sec-oracle-scope) is the paper's real contribution: exactness is what makes residuals attributable, and the C2-style scoping (parity certifies the chain from QC-derived intermediates, not full-path derivation) is precisely the discipline this literature lacks.
2. **Leakage hygiene beyond field norm**: nested outer/inner cross-fitting for both calibration and smearing, with unit tests that assert the inner-OOF population sizes (`tests/test_analysis_pipeline.py:280–371`). The OOF Duan smear is a genuine, sensible improvement over the in-sample original.
3. **Verified mathematics**: the piecewise log-linear quantile function, its analytic first moment with truncated Pareto tail, and the strict-crossing conventions are correct and MC-validated against closed forms; the browser inversion in `app.js:338–373` faithfully mirrors `predictive_process.py`, including the tail branch.
4. **The temporal factor protocol** is a clean, honest design, and reporting the unfactored numbers alongside — with the explicit statement that factor interpretation is unidentified — is exactly right.
5. **Consistent survey-weighting discipline** end to end, including Kish effective n in the shrinkage variances and delta-method ratio variances.
6. **Artifact and honesty infrastructure**: FACTS.md claim-to-artifact mapping, generated FINDINGS.md, staged atomic writes, SHA-256 provenance, byte-identical rerun claims, and the featured (rather than buried) NY validation failure. Ironically, the three most serious problems above are all places where the manuscript or the app *departs* from this machinery — which is an argument for the machinery.
