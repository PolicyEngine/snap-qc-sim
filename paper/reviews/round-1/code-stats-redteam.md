## Findings

**1. The tail scale (0.4674) is refuted by its own training data — the mean-excess function it assumes flat falls monotonically to ~0.26 at the attachment depth. Severity: blocker** (for every tail-dependent output).
`_fit_tail` estimates an exponential excess scale from residuals above their weighted *q90* (`analysis/distributional_deviation_model.py:194-199`) but attaches it above the conditional *q99* (`analysis/predictive_process.py:161-165, 236-242, 392-400`). Exponentiality implies a flat mean-excess function. Replicating the exact OOF median residuals (cutoff 1.638921, scale 0.467446 — identical to `distributional_results.json`), the training mean excess is 0.4674 (q90) → 0.3578 (q95) → 0.2973 (q97.5) → 0.2688 (q99) → 0.2625 (q99.5). The fitted scale is ~1.75× too heavy at the depth where it is used, an artifact of fitting at q90 and attaching at q99. Out of sample it is worse: FY2024 weighted mean excess beyond the shipped q99 is 0.154 (0.218 beyond q99+0.5, ~7 SE below 0.4674). Consequences: (a) 3.10% of FY2024 deviator mass lies beyond q99 (the model premises 1% — the repo's own q99 coverage row, 96.87%, states this without drawing the conclusion); (b) ~10.3% of the analytic expected error dollars sit in the extrapolated tail region; (c) the unbounded Pareto (α=2.14) routinely draws impossible values — one 53-state × 4,000-draw sweep produced 6,815 case-draws with |D| > $3,000 and 507 > $10,000, max **$95,252** for a single monthly case error, against a physical support bounded by the maximum allotment (training max |D| = $1,265; FY2024 max = $2,377). The in-browser sampler (`app/public/app.js:356-357`) inherits all of this. No tail goodness-of-fit diagnostic exists anywhere in the JSONs; docs list "assess tail stability" as future work while shipping the tail to the app. **Fix**: fit the excess scale at the attachment depth (exceedances over predicted conditional q99, or a cutoff-stable GPD fit with the shape reported), truncate at the physical allotment bound, and add the mean-excess-by-cutoff table to `distributional_results.json`; regenerate everything downstream.

**2. The enforced finiteness gate (scale < 1, finite mean) is not the load-bearing one; the shipped statistics need scale < 0.5 (finite variance), and even that leaves the reported SDs seed-unstable. Severity: major.**
Gates at `distributional_deviation_model.py:202-205`, `predictive_process.py:354-358`, `scripts_build_model_data.py:247-248` all enforce only scale < 1. Every uncertainty statistic actually published — the model SD column in FINDINGS' "Model process versus observed bootstrap" (`FINDINGS.md:160-165`), the app's SD tile and tier probabilities — requires finite variance, i.e., scale < 0.5. The fitted 0.4674 (α=2.14) sits 7% from that boundary: variance exists, but third/fourth moments diverge, so a 4,000-draw SD is a lottery. Across 12 seeds the fixed-roster SD ranges **0.763–1.667pp for CO** and **0.609–1.622pp for NY**; the JSON's single-seed values (CO 1.091, NY 0.616) are unreproducible-in-distribution point draws, and the table's CO-above/NY-below narrative is partly seed noise. A retrained scale of 0.51 would pass every existing gate while making the reported SDs estimates of an infinite quantity. **Fix**: assert scale < 0.5 with a margin (or report the tail index and its implication), report seed-averaged SDs with MC error, or compute the variance analytically (closed forms exist for the piecewise-log-linear body and the tail when scale < 0.5). Note this instability largely disappears once Finding 1 is fixed (scale ≈ 0.26 → α ≈ 3.8).

**3. The validation simulates a different process than the app runs, then presents two non-commensurate SDs side by side. Severity: major.**
The FINDINGS table's model column draws every fixed roster case once (process variance only; `distributional_deviation_model.py:498-540`, centering `official − observed_point + draw` at `:539`). The app's model mode bootstraps cases *and* redraws deviations (`app/public/app.js:348-372`) and centers at `official − model_mean` (`:375-384`). Neither the app's variance design nor its anchoring convention is validated anywhere; my replication gives materially different SDs between designs (CA 0.999 boot vs 0.878 fixed). The observed-bootstrap column, meanwhile, is sampling variance around fixed errors — a different variance component from the model column it sits next to; the caption (`FINDINGS.md:156-158`) describes both designs but the table invites reading the columns as the same estimand. **Fix**: validate the exact shipped configuration (bootstrap+redraw, model-mean anchor), seed-averaged, for all 53 states; label the components.

**4. Anchoring at the official rate hides level misses of up to 2.8× that propagate straight into the shipped SDs and tier probabilities — corr(log level-miss, log SD-miss) = 0.90. Severity: blocker for the app's model-mode claims.**
Per-state analytic dollar rates from the shipped export (method identical to the pipeline's own `expected_error_dollars`; matches the JSON's 4 published analytic points) versus observed FY2024 QC-sample dollar rates: model/observed = **0.495 (DC: 6.95 vs 14.03)**, 0.566 (GU), 0.572 (RI), 0.607 (GA), 0.609 (NY) at one end; **2.763 (ID)**, 2.554 (UT), 2.354 (WI), 2.035 (SD), 1.739 (WV) at the other. Equal-state dollar-rate MAE 1.86pp; national issuance-weighted model rate 6.22% vs observed 7.22% (−14%). Mean |anchor shift| 4.2pp, range −4.3 (WI) to **+21.5pp (AK**, whose shift is 30+ model SDs; mostly the official-vs-sample regression wedge of 19.9pp, plus model−sample −1.7pp). Because the error process is multiplicative and the anchor is additive, a state whose modeled dollars run k× observed keeps a spread ~k× wrong after anchoring: across 53 states, log(SD_model/SD_obs) tracks log(model/obs level) with r = **0.903**. Anchored tier probabilities deviate from observed-mode by >10pp exactly at boundary states — MT 14.6pp, GU 12.7pp, WV 11.0pp (e.g., MT model-mode: 20% chance of 5%-share tier and 13% of 15%-share vs observed-mode 12%/7%) — and are compressed (overconfident) in the under-modeled high-error states. The uncapped tail additionally inflates model-mode SDs ~12% at the median (capped-at-$2,500 sensitivity). **Fix**: multiplicative state calibration — the repo already builds exactly this machinery in the frozen FY2023-factor path; ship factors in `model_data.json` and apply them per case in-app — plus a per-state raw-gap disclosure and a gate that disables or flags model mode where the level ratio is outside, say, [0.7, 1.4] (AK, DC, GU, RI, GA, NY, ID, UT, WI, SD fail today).

**5. The distributional model is validated on crossing prevalence, but the app displays dollars; no per-state dollar-rate validation exists for 49 of 53 states. Severity: major.**
All state-calibration tables for the distributional route (`FINDINGS.md:143-154`; `_crossing_state_rates`, `distributional_deviation_model.py:340-380`) are case-crossing prevalence, on which the model looks calibrated (national 13.22% vs 13.39%). The quantity the app computes and displays — weighted error dollars over issuance — is validated only via the 4-state, single-seed simulation table. Prevalence conceals the magnitude misfit: nationally, dollars are −14% (Finding 4) while prevalence is −1%. **Fix**: add a 53-state dollar-rate calibration table (obs vs analytic model rate) for the distributional route to `distributional_results.json` and FINDINGS; it is a 20-line addition using functions that already exist.

## Verdicts

**(a) Analysis pipeline: sound-with-fixes.** Temporal hygiene, target choice, universe filtering, OOF/calibration mechanics, and the frozen-factor protocol survived hostile recomputation intact and reproducible. What does not survive: the tail parameter is misfit by its own training diagnostics (1), the finiteness gate guards the wrong moment and the published SDs are single-seed draws of a near-non-existent quantity (2), the route the pipeline validates best is validated on the wrong metric (prevalence, not dollars) (5), and the coverage section's framing understates a decisive one-sided misfit (6). All are fixable within the existing architecture; until 1–2 land, no downstream number that touches the tail (SDs, tail draws, expected dollars, the 4-state table) should be quoted.

**(b) The app's use of it: unsound as shipped for model mode's distinctive claims.** Observed mode is unaffected. Model mode's only additions over the anchor — spread and tier probabilities — are exactly what the level-miss corrupts (r = 0.90 coupling, tier distortions up to 15pp at the boundary states the tool exists to inform, SDs inflated ~12% by a refuted tail, +21.5pp silent anchor shift for AK), the mechanism it runs is validated nowhere (3), and its documentation asserts the integration doesn't exist (7) while pointing at per-state disclosures that don't exist (8). Minimum bar to flip this verdict: fix the tail (1), apply multiplicative state factors in the export/app (4/9), publish the 53-state dollar-rate gap table (5/8), and validate the shipped bootstrap+redraw configuration seed-stably (2/3).
