## Stylistic review: "Administrative quality control data as an oracle" 

Manuscript: `/Users/maxghenis/PolicyEngine/snap-qc-sim/paper/index.qmd` (467 lines; bibliography at `/Users/maxghenis/PolicyEngine/snap-qc-sim/paper/references.bib`)

### Recommendation: Major revisions

The underlying scholarship is unusually disciplined — claims are scoped, numbers are internally consistent (I verified the arithmetic: 6,081 = sum of seven state counts; 36,486 = 6,081 x 6; 37/283 = 13.1%; 5% x \$1.268B = \$63M), and the limitations section is a model of candor. The revisions demanded here are presentational and architectural, but they are not cosmetic: the paper currently has zero tables, zero figures, and zero display equations for a manuscript whose spine is a distributional model and a five-way state comparison; it narrates its own internal QA history in three places; one subsection reads as release engineering; and the literature apparatus (9 bibliography entries, one uncited) is far below venue norm. Each of these alone is fixable; together they keep a strong paper below the JPAM submission bar.

---

### Major issues

**1. No tables or figures — a defect, not a style choice.** The paper is prose+numbers throughout, and at several points the prose is forced to do a table's job, degrading readability and auditability:

- *Decomposition layers* (lines 215–246). Layer 1 gives Colorado strict/broad shares plus national strict/broad; Layer 2 gives a four-way split; Layer 3 gives a case-count partition. Eight-plus estimates across three non-nested definitions, held entirely in the reader's head. A single table (rows = layers/definitions; columns = Colorado share, national share, universe, units) would replace three bold-labeled paragraph blocks and expose the units inconsistency noted in Major issue 5.
- *Verification results by state* (lines 144–149). "6,081 cases in total (856 in Colorado; 847 New York; ...)" is an inline parenthetical doing a table's work.
- *Model validation* (lines 301–312). Nine quantile-coverage levels reported as prose ("seven of nine levels... 75th and 90th over-cover by 3.5 and 3.3 points"), plus a 2x2 of calibration MAEs (unfactored/factored x equal-/issuance-weighted). This is exactly a table.
- *Boundary-state tier probabilities* (lines 355–361) and *audit-volume expected values* (lines 363–374). Five states with probabilities, then six states with dollar figures, inline.
- At least one figure is close to obligatory: the simulated distribution of a boundary state's measured rate with tier cutoffs overlaid. Line 79–80 says the simulator "makes one point vividly that summary statistics obscure" — the manuscript then delegates the vividness to a website and presents the point as summary statistics. The paper must make its own central point visually. A calibration scatter (observed vs. predicted state rates, r = 0.906, line 311) is a second strong candidate.

**2. Documentation/project-report register in identified zones.**

- *"Certification as an artifact" (lines 194–205) reads as a release note.* Commit hashes (`de0efdc7`, `b53ce208`), a truncated binary SHA-256 (`bb8ec236…`), offline-rebuild narration, and throughput benchmarks (230 cases/s; 279/s "with compilation amortized") belong in the repository or a reproducibility appendix, not the body of a journal article. The scholarly content here is two sentences: (i) the toolchain is pinned and independently rebuilt, and (ii) runtime does not constrain the designs considered (~26 seconds for the seven-state suite). Compress to that; move hashes to the data-availability apparatus. The closing tagline is addressed under Major issue 4.
- *Internal audit narration appears three times*: the Introduction ("every headline number... survived an adversarial audit that forced a full correction round of an earlier pipeline," lines 88–92), footnote `[^audit]` (lines 271–277), and the Kentucky aside ("An earlier pipeline's dramatic Kentucky estimate (−5.3 points) dissolved under the corrected event definition," lines 331–333). The *substance* is publishable and already present as validation results — the target contrast (RAWBEN−BENFIX reconciles 99.997% vs. FSBEN's 83.65%, lines 265–269) and definition-sensitivity of the Kentucky contrast. Reframe those as target validation and a sensitivity check, respectively, and delete the process memoir. A reader cannot verify "an adversarial audit," and the history of the authors' own earlier wrong pipeline is not evidence about the world. If the audit deserves credit, that is what Acknowledgments are for. Footnote `[^audit]`'s closing self-assessment ("the audit-and-correct loop is, in our view, as much a part of the method as the models") should go.
- *Roadmap section (lines 390–413) is a software plan.* "Encoding the 'off' state of options whose current encodings are structural rather than scalar," "defining scenario semantics for states without the option," "the pieces now exist separately" — this is backlog language. The intellectual content (what blocks engine-computed counterfactuals, and that the cost-sharing formula itself is encodable) fits in one Discussion paragraph. Related: "until the counterfactual leg lands" (line 432) and "is implemented as future work in the same harness" (lines 190–191 — "implemented" and "future work" contradict each other; say "planned").
- *Tool-UI references*: "the mode's mechanism and its distinction... are labeled in the interface" (lines 350–351) and "an anchoring choice stated in the tool" (line 320). The paper should state the anchoring choice as its own methods decision (it does); how the website labels things is the website's documentation.

**3. Literature engagement is far below venue norm.** Nine bibliography entries; three are canonical methods cites (Koenker–Bassett, Duan, Efron), two are agency documents, one is the solver, two are rules-as-code, and one (`pedregosa2011sklearn`) is never cited in the text. Missing literatures a JPAM referee will demand:

- *Noisy performance measures in high-stakes assignment* — the tier-lottery finding has a direct antecedent in the school-accountability literature (Kane and Staiger's work on imprecise accountability measures) and hospital report cards. Not engaging this makes a well-known result look novel.
- *SNAP QC and payment-error empirics* — GAO and USDA OIG work on error rates, and the documented 2015–2017 QC data-quality episode, which line 123–124 waves at with "(episodically) liability consequences" and which directly supports the paper's own point (line 422–423) that reviewer behavior is part of the measurement regime. This claim needs citations, not an adverb.
- *Administrative burden* — the "information failure, not calculation failure" framing and the burden-intermediates features sit squarely in the Herd/Moynihan literature; zero engagement.
- *Rules-as-code beyond two cites* — OpenFisca and related benefit-calculation validation work.

**4. Bold-claim discipline: several sentences overreach the register.** The paper is mostly excellent on this (e.g., "accounting bounds, not causal estimates," line 380), which makes the lapses conspicuous:

- Line 204–205: "Verification at administrative scale is not a compute problem." A category-level pronouncement extrapolated from one laptop benchmark on one state. The preceding sentence already makes the quantified point; the tagline adds only swagger. Cut.
- Line 360–361: "sampling noise is worth more than most feasible policy improvements." "Most feasible policy improvements" is an unquantified comparator. Either support it (compare the sampling SD in points to the observed distribution of year-over-year state rate changes) or delete.
- Line 439: "the highest-value verification oracle available to rules-as-code work in the benefits space" — in the Introduction this superlative is hedged ("They are, we argue," line 56–58); in the Conclusion it is asserted as fact, with no comparison set ever established. Restore the hedge or establish the comparison.
- Lines 444–445: "we have tried to be as clear about what the models miss as what they capture. Both kinds of honesty are prerequisites..." — the paper praising its own honesty. Show, don't self-certify. (By contrast, "A measurement system designed for oversight now sets prices," line 435, and "The formula prices a noisy estimate as if it were the truth," line 360, are earned summaries backed by shown numbers — keep those.)
- Aphorism density generally: "The result is less interesting than the path to it" (line 157), "The oracle audits its own paperwork" (line 168), "Reproducibility claims should themselves be reproducible" (line 196), "A residue that is not ours" (line 169). One such line per paper is voice; four or five is a blog.

**5. Analytic-framing gaps a strict referee will raise (each fixable in a sentence or two, but currently absent):**

- *Development/evaluation reuse.* Early parity runs failed; two encoding defects were fixed against the oracle; the final result is exact match on the same 6,081 cases (lines 155–166). The exactness claim is therefore post-correction and in-sample. State explicitly that the fixes were global rule corrections rather than case-specific accommodations, and ideally report a confirmatory run on a held-out state or year. Relatedly, "independently encoded" (abstract, line 17) — independent of what? Say so.
- *Units mixing across layers.* Layers 1–2 are shares of error *dollars*; Layer 3 is a share of error *cases*. Line 244–246 splices them in one sentence ("roughly 3–10% of error dollars by layers 1–2, and bounded above by 13.1% in the replay"). Report a dollar-weighted replay share or flag the units switch.
- *Layer 2's universe is unstated* (lines 223–229): Colorado or national? Layer 1 specifies both; Layer 2 specifies neither.
- *No uncertainty on key point estimates.* 86.9% (n = 283 → binomial SE ≈ 2 pp), the AUCs, 47.8% precision — all reported bare. The paper quantifies sampling noise for states while reporting its own headline shares without intervals.
- *No formal model specification.* The deviation model (lines 290–299) — hurdle structure, sign model, nine monotonized conditional quantiles, exponential-in-logs tail, Duan smear — is compressed into one paragraph of prose with zero equations. A methods-friendly venue expects the model written down, and "shrunk by an empirical-Bayes precision rule" (line 308) is not reproducible from the text as stated; give the formula.

**6. Solo-authored paper written as "we."** One author is listed (line 4); "we" appears throughout, and the Acknowledgments say "Errors are ours" (line 467). Use "I," or resolve the authorship inconsistency the other direction.

### Minor issues (line-locatable)

1. **Line 10–29 (abstract).** Sentences 2, 3, and 5 each carry two or three ideas. Sentence 2 promises "two questions this paper treats as one," but the Introduction's roadmap is three uses (lines 60–82) — the framing is never cashed out. Sentence 5's main clause is infrastructure ("embed it in an open, in-browser Monte Carlo simulator") with the headline finding demoted to a subordinate "where..." clause: the abstract technically ends on a finding, but syntactically ends on the tool. Make the tier-lottery finding the final main clause and drop "open, in-browser" from the abstract. "Six asserted stages" (line 19) is test-harness vocabulary — "six intermediate computation stages." "86.9% of filtered error cases" (line 24) — "filtered" is opaque without the body; say "of error cases passing reconstruction filters." "Moves hundreds of millions of dollars per state per year" (line 14) overstates the typical state — the Introduction itself says tens of millions for a mid-sized state (lines 41–42).
2. **Acronyms.** SNAP is never expanded anywhere (title through conclusion). OBBBA is used at lines 78, 123 but never paired with "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" (line 34). "USDA FNS" (line 458) and "ROC AUC" (line 280) unexpanded. QC is properly introduced (line 48) — do the same for the rest.
3. **Line 36, 411:** legal citations lack section symbols ("7 U.S.C. § 2013(a)(2)"; "7 C.F.R. § 275.11").
4. **Lines 160–172:** the manuscript's only bullet list, with bold run-in labels and aphoristic fragments ("A residue that is not ours."). Convert to prose — the three findings classes are one paragraph each, or one paragraph total.
5. **Bold run-in headers throughout** (lines 109, 117, 123, 215, 223, 230, 280, 290, 301, 322, 339, 345, 355, 363, 376). At this density the device reads as structured notes. Several disappear naturally once the tables in Major issue 1 exist; convert the rest to plain topic sentences or numbered subsections. Also line 242–243: bolding an entire claim sentence in running text is not journal style.
6. **Line 34–35:** "rewired the incentives around a statistic that few people outside program-integrity offices had reason to watch" — unsupported color about reader attention; trim.
7. **Line 57–58:** *oracle* is used as a term of art with only an implicit definition. Define it explicitly on first use ("an oracle, in the software-testing sense: an external source of ground-truth outputs for given inputs").
8. **Line 142–144:** the artifact under test — the encodings and engine — gets one clause and a repo name. A reader outside the project needs a short paragraph: what the encodings are, their provenance, who wrote them, what "deterministic rules engine" means.
9. **Line 155:** heading "What reaching exactness taught" is blog register; likewise "The QC system and the money attached to it" (line 99) is looser than venue norm. (Heading *case* is fully compliant — sentence case throughout, including run-ins; no revision needed on that axis.)
10. **Line 214 vs. 126:** "\$1.268B" vs. "\$1.27B" for the same quantity; pick one precision.
11. **Line 232:** "the public \$3-shift reconstruction solver" — jargon never explained; one clause on what the \$3 shift is.
12. **Line 259–261:** code literals in running text (`CASE == 1`); prefer "the official case universe (CASE = 1)." The RAWBEN/BENFIX/FSBEN definitions are acceptable since defined inline.
13. **Line 296–297:** "scale 0.467, satisfying the finite-mean condition" — state the condition (scale < 1) rather than asserting satisfaction.
14. **Line 314:** "One validation failure is worth featuring rather than burying" — meta-commentary about the paper's own rhetoric; open with the finding itself.
15. **Footnotes.** Count and placement are disciplined (three). `[^repo]` (lines 95–97) duplicates the byte-identical/SHA-256 claim made again at lines 452–454 — state it once, in the availability section. `[^audit]` should shrink to the target-validation fact per Major issue 2. `[^delay]`'s closing editorial clause ("a detail that matters for any projection") is fine but trimmable. The simulator URL sits in running text (line 79) — footnote or availability section.
16. **References.bib:** no DOIs on any entry (Duan, Koenker–Bassett, Efron, Mérigoux, OECD all have them); `@misc` web entries lack access dates; `pedregosa2011sklearn` is in the bibliography but never cited — if scikit-learn produced the estimates, cite it in Methods (lines 280, 294), otherwise it will silently drop from the rendered bibliography.
17. **Line 450:** retitle "Reproducibility" to "Data and code availability" (venue convention) and consider ordering it after Acknowledgments.
18. **Currency dollar signs:** the .qmd relies on Pandoc's math-delimiter heuristics to keep "\$56," "\$38," "\$1.27B," "\$3-shift," etc. out of math mode (lines 103, 121, 126–129, 174, 232, 366–372, 385–386). It likely renders correctly today because every "$" is followed by a digit and preceded by a space, but it is fragile under edits — escape currency as `\$` throughout. (I note `paper/out/index.pdf` exists, so current rendering can be spot-checked.)
19. **Line 235–238:** "The solver and the engine partition the 283 cases identically, an independent cross-validation of both" — both consume the same file and the solver was adapted by the authors; "independent" needs a qualifying clause (distinct codebases, distinct logic).
20. **Lines 463–467, the closing flourish:** "Errors are ours — and, per the method, enumerable." Verdict: cut it. Beyond the "ours"/solo-author problem, the wit makes a claim the paper's own scope section refutes — the method enumerates *computation* errors within a bridged scope (lines 177–192); modeling errors, scope errors, and interpretation errors are precisely the non-enumerable ones the Limitations section catalogs. A joke that contradicts the paper's most careful paragraph is a liability in front of exactly the referees this paper courts. "All errors are my own." Also consider whether thanking "the maintainers of the axiom-oracles verification harness" requires a disclosure statement if the author or affiliated organizations maintain it — the relationship between PolicyEngine, the encodings, and the harness should be disclosed explicitly.

### Strengths

1. **Claim scoping of rare quality.** Section "Scope: what the parity result does and does not certify" (lines 177–192) preempts overreading of the exactness result; "accounting bounds, not causal estimates" (lines 380–384) and the anchoring disclosure (lines 314–320) do the same for the simulator. This is the discipline most submissions lack.
2. **The Limitations section (lines 415–432)** is genuinely exhaustive — solver filter attrition (22 of 305), the adjudicated-label caveat, the New York transport failure, fixed error process, descriptive-only contrasts — and each limitation traces to a specific design decision elsewhere in the paper.
3. **Featuring a validation failure** (New York, lines 314–320) rather than suppressing it, with the design consequence (official-rate anchoring) stated.
4. **Numeric internal consistency.** Every cross-checkable figure I tested reconciles across abstract, body, and sections (state counts, cell counts, percentages, tier arithmetic), and the fact-catalog practice (`paper/FACTS.md`, confirmed present) supports auditability.
5. **Signposting and heading case.** The three-uses roadmap (lines 60–82), consistent `@sec-` cross-referencing, and clean transitions ("Decomposition is retrospective," line 255) give the pipeline architecture real coherence; sentence case is followed without exception.
6. **The two-sided audit-volume finding (lines 363–374)** is the paper at its best: a non-obvious incentive asymmetry, stated sharply, with every claim priced in expectation and variance from shown simulations.

The paper's core problem is that its register oscillates between a first-rate empirical article and the changelog of the project that produced it. The revision path is clear: add the exhibits, formalize the model, excise the process memoir and taglines, engage the three missing literatures, and let the results — which are strong enough not to need slogans — carry the register.
