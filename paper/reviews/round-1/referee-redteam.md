## Red-Team Review: "Administrative quality control data as an oracle" (paper/index.qmd @ bce8eb6, origin/paper)

### Recommendation: Major revisions

Not reject: I ran 25+ independent verification attacks and the quantitative core reproduces to the digit almost everywhere it can be checked — including a full independent recomputation of the Colorado and national cause-code decomposition from the raw public QC file. The problems are overclaim, a sign-flipped validation result, and unverifiable headline components — serious, but of the fixable kind. Note: the working tree switched to `claims-fixes` mid-review; everything below refers to `origin/paper` (manuscript at `bce8eb6`, cert report as amended by `cea512b`), which I verified is byte-identical to what I read.

### Headline finding

The oracle section's universality claim is false by the repo's own data. `paper/index.qmd:143-145` asserts the encodings were tested "against **every fiscal 2024 QC case in those states**: 6,081 cases in total." The FY2024 QC file (SHA-pinned in the paper's own cert report) contains **6,194** CASE==1 cases in those seven states: NY 885 vs 847 verified, TX 955 vs 906, MD 745 vs 722, AZ 925 vs 922 — 113 cases (1.8%) excluded. The authors' own artifact stores both numbers side by side (`/Users/maxghenis/PolicyEngine/snap-qc-sim/app/public/data.json`, fields `n` vs `verified`), and the upstream oracle configs are careful to say "847 **in-scope** NY active-case reviews" (`axiom-oracles/comparisons/ny-snap-qc.yaml`). The manuscript deleted the qualifier. The exclusions are non-random — documented in `axiom-oracles/docs/snap-qc-oracle-playbook.md` §4 as SSI-CAP/MFIP standardized-benefit units (elderly/disabled SSI cases) plus missing-FSBEN/CERTHHSZ rows — so "every case" vs "every in-scope case" is not cosmetic for a coverage claim. "No mismatches, no missing values" (`index.qmd:151-153`) compounds it: missing-value cases were excluded upstream of the denominator.

### Major issues

**1. "Every fiscal 2024 QC case" is false for four of seven states** (`paper/index.qmd:143-145`, abstract lines 18-19, conclusion line 442). Evidence above. Fix is one clause: state the in-scope universe and the 113 documented exclusions. FACTS.md C1 correctly avoids the universality claim; the manuscript added it.

**2. The parity target is misdescribed; the scope caveat (sec-oracle-scope) is incomplete.** The manuscript frames the oracle as "real cases, **adjudicated by the agency's own reviewers**, with inputs and outputs recorded" and as categorically stronger than cross-model comparisons (`index.qmd:135-139`), and later says "once the encodings reproduce the **reviewer arithmetic** exactly" (`index.qmd:169-170`). The upstream harness documents the opposite framing: the compared benefit is **FSBEN, the FNS/Mathematica QC Minimodel's software recomputation** — "FSBEN is not the benefit the household received" — from inputs that Mathematica **edits until the calculated benefit matches the raw benefit within $5**, adjusting the dependent-care, excess-shelter, and medical deductions in sequence (playbook §2, citing techdoc ch. III.B; `comparisons/ny-snap-qc.yaml`: "compares the constructed benefit (FSBEN)... this is admin-grade benefit-computation parity"). So parity certifies agreement with another program's arithmetic on machine-reconciled input vectors — meaningful, but much closer to the "cross-model comparison" the paper disparages at `index.qmd:134-136` than to reviewer adjudication. `sec-oracle-scope` (`index.qmd:177-192`) discloses only the supplied-intermediates caveat; it says "we state the scope precisely" while omitting the FSBEN/Minimodel target, the $5 consistency-editing cascade, and the eligibility-gates-fed-passing-defaults convention. The authors demonstrably know the FSBEN distinction — `index.qmd:266-269` rejects FSBEN as a deviation target for exactly this constructedness. The abstract also carries the parity claim with no caveat at all, violating the paper's own rule (FACTS.md §G: "Parity claims always carry the C2 scope caveat").

**3. The abstract's 86.9% replay claim has no artifact anywhere.** `index.qmd:23-24, 231-240` and FACTS.md D4 cite "amterr lab ANALYSIS (snapshot)" and `reconstruct_co_fy2024.R`. Neither exists: `paper/snapshot/` contains only `cert/CERT_REPORT.md` and three labs JSONs (none contain 246/283/37); no commit in this repo's entire history ever contained them (verified via `git log --all --diff-filter=A`); `TheAxiomFoundation/axiom-oracles` has no such files; and the public `giannella/snap_qc` repo contains the generic reconstruction script but no FY2024 Colorado adaptation. The 246/283 partition, the 37-case residual, and the "solver and engine partition the 283 cases identically" cross-validation are therefore unverifiable assertions — directly contradicting `index.qmd:84` ("Everything here is reproducible from public data") and the Reproducibility section's fact-catalog claim, and defeating FACTS.md's stated purpose for its own row. Mitigation that lowers (but does not remove) fabrication risk: I independently recomputed the sibling Layer 1 numbers from the raw QC CSV and they reproduce **exactly** — CO 305 cases, $112.6M, strict $8.2M (7.3%), broad $11.9M (10.5%); national $8.13B, $320M (3.9%), $1.50B (18.4%) — and Layer 2's four-class split matches `phase_a_classification.json` to the decimal. The same lab plausibly produced Layer 3. But the paper's second-strongest abstract sentence currently rests on an uncommitted artifact. Commit the replay output (case-level, like `phase_a_classification.json`) or cut the number from the abstract.

**4. A featured validation result is sign-flipped against its own artifact.** `index.qmd:301-304`: the 75th and 90th percentiles "**over-cover** by 3.5 and 3.3 points — the model slightly **overstates** mid-upper magnitudes." The artifact says the opposite: weighted coverage 71.53% at nominal 75% and 86.75% at nominal 90% (`analysis/FINDINGS.md:133-134`; `distributional_results.json`), i.e., **under**-coverage of −3.47 and −3.25pp, meaning the predicted quantiles sit too low and the model **understates** mid-upper magnitudes. FACTS.md E3 records the minus signs correctly; the manuscript inverted them. The direction matters: understated upper-mid magnitudes push simulated measured rates down — the same direction as the NY underprediction the paper does feature — so the flip converts a coherent downward-bias story into a false reassurance. Also consistent with the same paragraph's own $183 predicted vs $189 observed.

**5. The boundary-state coin-flip percentages are untraceable for two of five states, and FACTS.md F1 certifies the untraceable values.** `index.qmd:356-359` and FACTS F1 claim flip probabilities ND 49%, WA 48%, CO 47%, KS 47%, NV 47%. Against every committed artifact: CO is **48%** (48.04 in `paper/snapshot/labs/tier_noise_monte_carlo.json`; 48.47 in `mc_results_by_state.json`; 48.17 in `examples/results_by_state.json`) and NV is **46%** (45.78 / 45.89 / 46.33). ND's 49% matches two of three artifacts (48.82, 48.95) but not the tier-noise snapshot (48.42). Magnitude is ~1pp and the qualitative claim ("nearly even odds") survives, but the errors are not even directionally consistent (CO understated, NV overstated), the paper's ordering (WA above CO) contradicts two of three artifacts, and this is precisely the failure mode FACTS.md exists to prevent — its "every quantitative claim traces to a row; every row names its artifact" contract fails on F1, D2, and D4.

### Falsification attempt on the headline claim

The strongest available attack on "6,081 cases, six stages, zero tolerance, exact" is scope, not truth: it is not every case (issue 1), the right leg is a Minimodel recomputation on consistency-edited inputs (issue 2), and several intermediates are supplied (disclosed). I could not falsify the exactness itself: the CO leg was independently re-executed to byte-identical evidence under pinned SHAs (CERT_REPORT plus the `cea512b` second-auditor addendum), the seven per-state suite reports are committed publicly with exactly the claimed counts and zero mismatch concepts, 6,081×6=36,486 and 856×6=5,136 check, and the external narrative chain verifies (axiom-oracles #244 "95.3% exact" → rulespec-us #826 rounding fix, merged 2026-07-11 → #268 "856/856", #269 NY/CA; all real, dates coherent). For the 86.9%: I can neither falsify nor verify — that is the problem (issue 3).
