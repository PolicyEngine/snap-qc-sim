### Critical issues

1. **[MAJOR] Unexplained mechanism linking the named fixes to the coverage deterioration** (`paper/index.qmd:401-407`). The sentence attributes the starker coverage to the fix round's three named changes (tail refit at attachment depth, physical caps, frozen FY2023 factors). In the committed code, none of the three enters the nine coverage cells: coverage compares observed log|D| to the raw rearranged conditional quantiles (`analysis/distributional_deviation_model.py:437-442`, quantiles built at `:357-368` with no smear, tail, cap, or factor); the tail scale enters only survival and expected dollars (`:394-422`); factors multiply capped error dollars only (`:1446-1452`); and the cap rule `max(BENMAX, |RAWBEN − BENFIX|)` per case-year (`:945`, `:1271`) is by construction at least the case's own observed |D|, so capping can never flip an observed ≤ predicted indicator at any level — this also refutes the specific hypothesis that caps mechanically clamp predicted quantiles below observations. The archived pre-correction record (`paper/reviews/round-2/round2-methodology.md:18`: gaps −0.30 to −3.47, all negative, 7/9 within 3pp) shows the deterioration is concentrated at q.25–q.75 (−3.85, −6.10, −6.89 post-fix), levels no tail-attachment correction can reach. The actual channel must be something the sentence does not name — the PR #13 refit of the quantile stage itself and/or a corrected coverage evaluation in the pre-#13 code (FACTS E3, `paper/FACTS.md:53`, records the old tail scale 0.4674 but not the channel). Name the channel or decouple the coverage result from the three-item fix list; as written, the em-dash construction asserts a cause the committed mechanics cannot support.

### Minor issues

1. **[MINOR] "Made the remaining one starker" — direction honest, comparison incomplete** (`paper/index.qmd:403-404`). On the parent question of worsened-vs-revealed: the text already concedes causation in the worsening direction ("made ... starker"; "which the corrected pipeline no longer attains", `:411`), and "remaining" is factually supported — the pre-correction gaps were already one-sided negative (−0.30 to −3.47, `round2-methodology.md:18`), so the defect did pre-exist. But the manuscript discloses only "seven of nine levels within 3 points" for the earlier version; printing the pre-fix signed range would (a) let readers verify "remaining" (same one-sided pattern) and quantify "starker" (max gap roughly doubled, 3.47 → 7.44pp), and (b) remove the residual ambiguity of "starker" between "larger as measured" and "more clearly visible." This disclosure would strengthen, not weaken, the paper's own claim.
2. **[MINOR] "Structural rather than an estimator artifact" overreaches a two-estimator design** (`paper/index.qmd:414-415`). The comparison varies only the conditional-quantile estimator while sharing everything else: the deviation-probability stage is literally fit once and shared (`analysis/qrf_benchmark_results.json:3-4`, `fit_count_across_benchmark: 1`, `shared_predictions_between_estimators: true`), and both estimators share features, training years (2017–2019, 2022), the log|D| target, the cap and factor protocol (`analysis/QRF_BENCHMARK.md:83`), and the FY2024 evaluation — and both are tree ensembles. Persistence under both rules out a GBM-quantile-loss-specific artifact; it cannot rule out causes shared by the pipeline (feature set, tree-ensemble family, or the train-to-FY2024 distribution shift). The accurate weaker claim: the failure is not specific to the gradient-boosted quantile stack and is consistent with a cause shared across the protocol. "Indicates" hedges, and the decision consequence (retain GBM, gate deployment) is conservative under either reading, which is why this is minor rather than major.
3. **[MINOR] "Sign disagreement" rests on point estimates in two of three variants; the robust form goes unstated** (`paper/index.qmd:570-575`). With CIs spanning zero in the floor and point variants, the model-implied sign is not statistically resolved there; what IS resolved is that all three intervals exclude +$7.1M (max upper bound +$0.64M, `counterfactual_co_smd.json:196-199`, `:278-281`, `:360-363`) — the disagreement with the accounting-reverse bound survives the intervals even where the sign does not. Stating the interval-exclusion form would make the headline claim robust. Relatedly, the printed "−$1.6M to −$2.2M" is a range across censoring variants, not a confidence interval — the actual CIs are roughly twice as wide — and the CIs are conditional on fitted models with enumerated omitted uncertainty (`:52-53`); a clause distinguishing variant-range from interval would prevent misreading.
4. **[MINOR] Import the artifact's own counterintuitive-sign disclaimer** (`paper/index.qmd:576-577`). "The other predicts how re-adjudication risk shifts when the documentation feature flips" is the one clause readable as treating the model leg as a valid counterfactual predictor. The committed artifact explicitly warns that the fitted models imply lower error under SMD-off even as the documentation proxy rises, and that "this sign is not evidence that documentation requirements reduce errors" (`counterfactual_co_smd.json:370`). The paragraph never claims the model's sign is correct — good — but it also never carries this warning; one clause closes the gap.
5. **[MINOR] Ground the retirement argument explicitly in the mechanism mismatch** (`paper/index.qmd:575-580`). A sign disagreement between two non-causal constructs is symmetric: it shows they differ, not which is right. As written, the sentence partially escapes this because the "answer different questions" premise is in-sentence — the levers scale an accounting identity while the simulator's framing poses a counterfactual question — and the paper elsewhere supplies the asymmetry (accounting bounds are "an upper-bound identity rather than a behavioral prediction," `:542-545`; the surviving model scenario ships gated, with CIs, labeled an association, `:420-426`, FACTS I7). But the clause "their disagreement on a real case is the concrete argument for retiring accounting levers" states the disagreement as the argument, which invites the reading that the model won by disagreeing. One clause pinning retirement to the identity-versus-prediction mismatch (with the disagreement as the demonstration that the substitution is not innocuous), plus a pointer to why the model scenario is nonetheless servable, forecloses the misreading.
6. **[NIT] Identify the model leg behind the cost-share headline.** The −$1.6M..−$2.2M translation is driven by the direct crossing classifier's delta applied as a level shift (`counterfactual_co_smd.json:60`; shifted level 9.9183 = 9.97 − 0.0517 at `:180`), and that classifier is an uncalibrated raw probability (`:139`). The paper says only "the error model"; naming the leg would make the number reproducible from the text.
7. **[NIT] Two different "state MAE with frozen factors" figures co-occur in the changed region**: 0.885pp (official-error-rate model, @tbl-validate, `paper/index.qmd:430`) and 0.928pp (dollar-rate distributional model, QRF sentence, `:419`). Both are correct for their objects (FACTS E5 vs `qrf_benchmark_results.json:5020`); labeling the QRF one as the dollar-rate MAE would prevent an apparent inconsistency.
8. **[NIT] Errata discoverability.** "The fact catalog records the change" (`paper/index.qmd:411-412`) could name the entry (FACTS E3, PR #13); the paper also omits the pre-fix tail scale (0.4674 → 0.271), which would quantify the tail defect.

### Strengths

1. **Exact artifact fidelity.** Every quantitative claim I checked in the changed regions matches its committed artifact, usually to the printed digit, including the awkward ones (the coverage failure, the QRF's win on the coverage primary, the CIs spanning zero). The benchmark's shipped-GBM equivalence gate (`qrf_benchmark_results.json:4706-4711`) and determinism block (`:33-56`) make the comparison auditable.
2. **The QRF characterization is not cherry-picked** (parent question 1d). The forest's coverage win leads the sentence; both prespecified primary metrics are reported with honest direction; the losses cited are real. Omissions are secondary and roughly balanced across estimators (QRF's simulation-SD wins in three of four states, `:5038-5081`, and export-size wins; GBM's 10× runtime win) — a completeness nit, not a bias.
3. **Errata practice is adequate** (parent question 1c): in-text disclosure naming the superseded figure, a SUPERSEDES entry with PR reference in the fact catalog (`paper/FACTS.md:53`), a standing prohibition on pre-correction numbers (`:75`), and — verified by sweep — no stale coverage claims elsewhere in the manuscript (abstract's "report its validation failures alongside its results" is consistent; `index.qmd:216` and `:290` are unrelated hits).
4. **The reverse-bound comparison is the right one.** Using the +$7.12M direction-reversed bound (`counterfactual_co_smd.json:99-116`) rather than the −$7.38M adoption bound for the same flip shows care about tier nonlinearity and comparison direction.
5. **The limitations sentence is accurate and correctly conveys persistence** (parent question 3): "all nine coverage gaps remain one-sided negative after the tail and level-factor fixes" (`paper/index.qmd:605-607`) is verified both post-fix (artifact `one_sided_pattern`) and — critically for the word "remain" — pre-fix (all-negative per the round-2 record), and it correctly drives the deployment gating (seven jurisdictions outside [0.7, 1.4], `distributional_results.json:831-847`). "Every simulation result in this paper uses the observed-resample engine" (`:610`) holds even for the roadmap numbers, where the model enters only as a level shift on the observed bootstrap.

### Comparison to literature
The changed regions strengthen two practices worth noting: a prespecified estimator-benchmark decision rule with a machine-checked equivalence gate to the shipped model (rare in applied microsimulation validation), and in-text errata with a superseding fact catalog. The sign-disagreement finding — accounting identities versus model-implied counterfactuals diverging on a real case — is a genuinely useful cautionary result for the error-rate-simulation literature, provided the retirement logic is stated as mechanism honesty rather than implied model correctness.

### Questions for authors
1. What specific change in the PR #13 round moved the q.25–q.75 coverage cells from within roughly 3.5pp to 6–7pp under-coverage, given that the tail attachment, the physical cap, and the state factors do not enter the coverage computation in the committed code? Was the pre-#13 coverage evaluated against tail-blended or smear-adjusted quantiles, or did the refit itself change the quantile fits?
2. The cost-share translation feeds the direct classifier's raw (uncalibrated) probability delta as a level shift; the deployed scenario export reconciles against both legs (FACTS I6). Why is the uncalibrated leg the one behind the paper's headline range, and should the text say so?
3. For "structural rather than an estimator artifact": would a non-tree estimator family (e.g., linear quantile regression or a conformalized baseline) on the same protocol be a cheap third point before the fiscal 2025 confirmatory run?

Verdict on the three parent questions in brief: (1a) the framing is honest in direction — "made ... starker" already concedes the correction increased the measured failure, and "remaining" is documented — but the mechanism attribution is unsupported (the one MAJOR finding) and the pre-fix range should be printed; the caps-clamp-coverage hypothesis specifically is refuted by the committed cap rule and coverage code. (1b) "Structural" should weaken to "not specific to the gradient-boosted quantile stack." (2a–c) No sentence claims the model's sign is correct, but the retirement clause and the missing counterintuitive-sign disclaimer leave two readable gaps, each closable in one clause; the interval-exclusion of +$7.1M is the paper's strongest unstated evidence. (3) The limitations sentence is accurate as written.

## Editorial resolution note (author, appended at archive time)

The MAJOR's open question — what actually moved the coverage cells — was resolved by
reading the PR #13 diff of `analysis/distributional_deviation_model.py`: the pre-#13
pipeline computed `coverage = weighted_quantile_coverage(predicted_2024)` on the
*primary* model ("fitting primary signed distribution through FY2023"), while #13
computes `coverage = weighted_quantile_coverage(frozen_2024)` on the *frozen*
through-FY2022 configuration that the deployed export actually uses (FY2023 held out
for factor fitting). The manuscript fix names this evaluation-frame change as the
channel and decouples the coverage figures from the three-item fix list.
