# Voice bible — the migrations paper

Read before writing any section. Companion to FACTS.md, GROUND_TRUTH.md,
BEATS.md. This paper has its own voice; it does not inherit the
simulation paper's, and writers do not open that paper's prose.

## Who is reading

An applied economist or public-administration scholar who knows
synthetic control and cares about identification, and a benefits-
program official who has lived through a system migration and wants
to know what the record says happened. Write for the first reader's
standards in the second reader's words. Assume they know what SNAP is,
what a QC review is roughly, and what a p-value is; do not assume they
know cause codes, the tolerance threshold, or what "computing
apparatus" means — define each once, on first use, in a clause.

## Sentence laws

- Active voice, always. The actor is the subject: "reviewers code each
  error case," "the design refuses attribution," "Rhode Island's
  dollars rise." Be-verb + complement counts as passive; rewrite it.
- About twenty words is a ceiling, not a target. Vary rhythm: a short
  punch at most once per paragraph, earned; otherwise connected clauses
  that carry one thought together ("...rises \$2.90 per case-month
  against its donor, and the rise concentrates in the years FNS later
  billed").
- Numbers are protagonists. State the number, then what it is: "\$2.90
  per weighted case-month (permutation p = 0.023)". Never an adjective
  where a number can stand ("substantially" → the number).
- Present tense for the artifact and its findings ("the protocol
  fixes," "the estimate is"); past tense only for events in the world
  (UHIP launched; FNS billed).
- First sentences carry the most weight — of the paper, of every
  section, of every paragraph. Open with the claim or the object, never
  with orientation ("In this section we…", "It is worth noting…").
- Plain words. Migration, launch, replacement, error dollars, donor,
  placebo. Not "leverage," "robust to," "nuanced," "landscape."

## Signature moves (use)

- Time-anchored specificity: the September 2016 go-live; FY2017–19;
  the December 2019 end of the billed window.
- Self-implication: the paper's own design has a documented
  sensitivity (the joint donor fit); state it flat, in the results, not
  buried in limitations, and state what the second estimator does
  about it.
- Counterintuitive findings stated flat: Kentucky's null under a
  documented troubled launch; Oregon's refusal; the placebo that fires
  in one run and not the other.
- Scope carve-outs as strength: "This estimates a bundled replacement
  as implemented" is the claim's precision, not an apology. Never
  follow it with "however."

## Named anti-tics (kill on sight)

- Colon-labeled openers ("The point:", "The finding:", "The short
  version:").
- Inanimate carriers doing rhetoric ("the results carry the
  argument," "this section delivers"). Things do not "speak to,"
  "highlight," or "underscore."
- "X, not Y" with an invented negative pole. Negation only when Y is
  real information a reader might otherwise believe (the estimand
  sentence earns its "never," once).
- Empty triads and symmetric closers. Lists of three for cadence.
- Process narration and review ceremony: no "after N rounds," no
  "we corrected," no revision history. Frozen protocols ARE the
  paper's checkable property — say "the protocol was frozen before
  estimation" once per design, as a fact about the artifact.
- Defensive framing: no "one might object," no pre-arguing with a
  skeptic, no "prototype," no "preliminary" as a hedge (state what was
  and was not run instead).
- Duration as rigor ("months of…"). Credit the design, not the clock.
- Hedges stacked on stated uncertainty ("may possibly suggest").
  Uncertainty is a number and a rule; write the number and the rule.
- Em-dash chains. One per paragraph at most.
- Advocacy or design advice, including implied ("states should…",
  "this argues for…"). The paper takes no position on any policy or
  procurement choice. Neutrality shows by absence, never by
  announcement.

## Rationing

- At most two one-line punch paragraphs in the whole paper.
- At most one structured list per section; results go in tables.
- One quiet literate touch, if any, in the whole paper. Not two.

## Language locks (verbatim; do not paraphrase)

- Estimand: "bundled system replacement as implemented."
- Never: "the effect of a rules engine"; "the effect of software."
- Channels: "how the QC record classified the failures" (coding
  practice), not what the system mechanically did.
- Consequence window: "descriptive" and "verdict-inert."
- Decomposition: both estimators reported; the reproduction check is
  the bridge; neither is privileged.

## Calibration exemplar (written fresh; the register to match)

Rhode Island replaced its eligibility system in September 2016. Over
the next three fiscal years, the state's quality-control reviewers
found \$6.9 million, \$3.4 million, and \$6.3 million a year in error
dollars they attributed to the computing apparatus — up from \$0.4
million the year before. Against a synthetic Rhode Island built from
states that never migrated, that is \$2.90 per weighted case-month
(permutation p = 0.023), and the rise concentrates in exactly the
years for which the Food and Nutrition Service later billed the state
\$37.3 million. The billing record played no part in the estimate.
