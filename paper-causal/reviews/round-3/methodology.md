# Methodology review

Scope: `migrations-rev2` at `7c16ad28e66ff5fd701448f1d1e23d5fc2fbd047`. Line citations refer to `paper-causal/index.qmd` at that commit.

## Findings

1. **blocker — CONFIRMED — The paper mislabels 0.0732 as a familywise false-positive load for the design.** Lines 112–118 correctly calculate the probability of at least one *strict-outcome rank 1* under independent uniform ranks, but line 116 calls it “the design's own false-positive load.” The protocol-defined `signal` is a conjunction: strict p < 0.10 and client-placebo p >= 0.10 (lines 103–104, 109). Therefore 0.0732 is neither the FWER of the three unit verdicts nor the probability of at least one false `signal`; it ignores the placebo gate and tests the special event “rank 1,” not rejection under the unit rule. The same over-labeling recurs at line 261. The arithmetic is exact: `1 - (42/43)^2(34/35) = 0.073207...`, but the interpretation is not.

2. **blocker — CONFIRMED — The claimed resolution statement gives inferential work to a verdict-inert descriptive profile.** Line 134 says the design distinguishes RI's floor p from “the most extreme of 43” through the placebo condition and pre-named window profile. But p = 1/43 means exactly that RI is the most extreme of 43 for the strict statistic; neither another outcome nor a descriptive profile creates finer strict-test resolution. More importantly, lines 103–104 define the verdict using only strict p and client p, while lines 104 and 138 call the window profile descriptive and verdict-inert. The placebo can gate the verdict, but the window cannot distinguish the floor p inferentially. The addendum repeats the contradiction at lines 35–38 of `analysis/EVENT_FAMILY_ADDENDUM.md`.

3. **blocker — PLAUSIBLE — “Frozen addendum” risks laundering a post-results comparison into a preregistered familywise analysis.** Lines 106–108 call the addendum frozen without disclosing that commit `109da05` froze it after the parent results already existed; the abstract (line 8), RI results (line 134), limitations (line 261), and conclusion (line 279) then foreground RI as below the Bonferroni threshold. The manuscript does say neither parent protocol corrected across units and that unit verdicts remain frozen (lines 108–119), which prevents a fully confirmed laundering finding. Still, the addendum itself says it “pre-registers” reporting (`EVENT_FAMILY_ADDENDUM.md`, lines 3–7), an untenable term for an analysis choice made with committed ranks in hand. The manuscript must state the post-results timing wherever it characterizes the addendum's status; otherwise readers can reasonably infer ex-ante family specification.

4. **major — CONFIRMED — The independence benchmark omits the concrete dependence mechanism and is not design-based for these data.** Lines 112–115 and 261 state independence as an assumption, but the manuscript never tells readers that RI and KY use the same 42-jurisdiction donor pool and overlapping fiscal panel, while Oregon's donor pool substantially overlaps it. Synthetic-control pseudo-treated fits therefore reuse outcomes and jurisdictions, making cross-unit ranks dependent under the actual design. Uniform marginal ranks under exchangeability can be a randomization-inference premise, but independence does not follow. Thus 0.0732 is only an independence benchmark, not a familywise probability supplied by the permutation design. A joint permutation/randomization family procedure would address dependence if a defensible joint assignment mechanism can be specified; absent that, Bonferroni/Holm is the valid dependence-robust comparison.

5. **major — CONFIRMED — The paper does not justify why the event family excludes the three inferential channel tests.** The decomposition protocol fixes three inferential tests—`mass_change`, `disregard`, and `defect_or_mass_change`—and already applies its own 0.10/3 adjustment (`UHIP_DECOMPOSITION_PROTOCOL.md`, lines 104–115). Treating the three unit tests and three conditional RI decomposition tests as separate families can be defensible because they answer different questions and the decomposition follows the parent RI finding. But revision 2 simply declares “the three unit-level tests form a family” (line 106) and never states that hierarchy or why the confirmatory universe is 3 rather than 6. If all six claims belong to one confirmatory family, Bonferroni is 0.10/6 = 0.0167 and no floor p can clear it. If the channel tests form a separate, conditional family, the paper needs to say so and retain their already-frozen within-channel correction.

6. **major — CONFIRMED — Bonferroni is valid, but its presentation overstates what the floor establishes.** Bonferroni controls FWER under arbitrary dependence and is therefore safer here than the independence product. Holm is uniformly at least as powerful and yields the same result for these ordered unit p-values: RI 1/43 < 0.10/3, then KY 13/43 > 0.10/2. RI's Bonferroni-adjusted p is `3/43 = 0.0698`, not new sub-floor evidence. “Clears” is mathematically true but, repeated in the abstract, results, limitations, and conclusion (lines 8, 134, 261, 279), invites a strength reading from an ex-post threshold comparison. The paper should name this as a post-results sensitivity/comparison and emphasize adjusted p = 0.0698 alongside rank 1/43.

7. **minor — CONFIRMED — The rounded familywise quantity is internally consistent but less precise than the frozen addendum.** The addendum fixes 0.0732; manuscript lines 115, 134, and 261 report 0.073. This is ordinary three-decimal rounding, not a wrong number, but “fixes what the paper reports” would be cleaner if precision were consistent.
