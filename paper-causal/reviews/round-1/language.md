1. **major — CONFIRMED — The introduction duplicates most later results instead of only establishing the question.** Lines 44–81 preview every event result, the RI window profile, the decomposition, the donor-fit sensitivity, the reproduction check, and the estimator conclusion. Sections beginning at lines 144, 163, 180, 202, 228, and 254 then repeat those beats, often nearly verbatim. This creates substantial duplicated beats across sections.

2. **minor — CONFIRMED — Passive or be-verb-plus-complement constructions recur despite the “always” rule.** Representative instances include “each estimated” (line 8), “Each is estimated” (36), “was frozen” (125), “is permutation” (129), “is conditional” (129), “is reported”/“are estimated” formulations throughout the decomposition, and “are not re-estimated” in the protocol-facing discussion. 6. **minor — CONFIRMED — The abstract contains an em-dash chain.** Its single paragraph uses paired em-dash insertions for the three events and later carries several semicolon-separated result clauses, exceeding the one-em-dash-per-paragraph ration and producing the prohibited chain effect. 7. **minor — PLAUSIBLE — Several first sentences orient rather than state the section’s result.** “The events are...” (34), “Two frozen protocols govern...” (125), “Two further layers describe...” (230), and “Each event has one treated state...” (238) frame what follows. 8. **minor — CONFIRMED — “One signal, one null, one refusal” is a symmetric closer and cadence triad.** It appears at lines 54–55 and returns in the conclusion, contrary to the bans on empty triads and symmetric closers; it also substitutes informal labels for the exact artifact verdict.
